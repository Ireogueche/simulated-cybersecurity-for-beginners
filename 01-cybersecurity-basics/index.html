Cybersecurity Fundamentals

Explore the principles, terminology and practices that underpin cybersecurity. Learn how to identify risks, understand security controls and protect digital assets.

01. What is cybersecurity?

Protecting digital systems and information

Cybersecurity is the practice of protecting computers, networks, applications and information against unauthorised access, disruption, alteration or destruction.

It involves people, processes and technology. Strong cybersecurity is not achieved by installing one security product. It requires multiple safeguards, good decisions and continuous monitoring.

Example

A school protects student records by restricting access, using strong authentication, maintaining backups and training staff to recognise phishing.

02. The CIA triad

The CIA triad is a foundational model used to describe three important goals of information security.

Confidentiality

Information should only be accessible to people and systems that are authorised to view it.

Example

Student records are protected with access permissions so unauthorised people cannot read private information.

Integrity

Information should remain accurate and protected against unauthorised changes.

Example

Only authorised staff can update examination results, and changes are recorded for review.

Availability

Systems and information should be accessible when authorised users need them.

Example

Regular backups and recovery plans help a school restore access after a system failure.

03. Essential security terminology

Understanding these terms helps security professionals describe problems accurately and decide how to address them.

Asset

Something valuable that needs protection, such as a laptop, customer database or network.

Threat

A potential cause of harm, such as ransomware, a malicious insider or accidental data loss.

Vulnerability

A weakness that could be exploited, such as outdated software or weak access controls.

Risk

The possibility and potential impact of a threat exploiting a vulnerability.

Security control

A safeguard that reduces risk, such as encryption, backups or multi-factor authentication.

Attack surface

The points where a system could potentially be accessed or attacked.

04. Common security threats

Organisations face different kinds of threats. Each threat can affect confidentiality, integrity or availability.

Phishing

Deceptive messages that attempt to steal credentials, financial information or other data.

Malware

Malicious software that can damage systems, disrupt operations or expose information.

Insider threats

Security risks caused by people with legitimate access, whether through malicious actions or mistakes.

Weak authentication

Poor passwords or missing additional verification can make accounts easier to compromise.

Denial of service

Attacks that overwhelm a service and prevent legitimate users from accessing it.

Misconfiguration

Incorrect system settings that may expose services or information unnecessarily.

05. Defence in depth

Defence in depth uses multiple complementary security layers. If one control fails, other controls can still reduce the likelihood or impact of an incident.

Think in layers, not single solutions

Physical security

Locks, secure rooms, device protection and controlled facility access.

Identity and access

Unique accounts, least privilege and multi-factor authentication.

Network and endpoint security

Firewalls, endpoint protection, network segmentation and secure configurations.

Data protection

Encryption, backups, access restrictions and appropriate retention policies.

Monitoring and response

Reviewing security events, reporting incidents and following recovery procedures.

Practical Lab 01 — Security Risk Assessment

SCENARIO-BASED INVESTIGATION · BEGINNER LEVEL

YOUR ROLE: JUNIOR SECURITY ANALYST

Case file: Greenfield Training Centre

Greenfield is a small training centre that stores student records on an office laptop. Staff use the same wireless network for administrative work and guest internet access.

  • Asset: Laptop containing student records.
  • Finding 01: The router still uses its default administrator password.
  • Finding 02: Software updates have been delayed.
  • Finding 03: Guest devices share the staff network.
  • Finding 04: The laptop has no recent verified backup.

Investigation 1 of 3: Which asset needs protection?

Investigation 2 of 3: Which finding represents a vulnerability?

Investigation 3 of 3: Which action is the strongest immediate improvement to network access control?

Professional takeaway

Good security begins with identifying valuable assets, understanding threats and vulnerabilities, assessing risk and selecting suitable controls. Security is an ongoing process, not a one-time installation.
